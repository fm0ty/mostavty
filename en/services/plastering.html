Plastering and renders

Skim plastering, levelling and straightening walls, renovating and lime renders. The result is a flat wall with no waves that you can paint straight away.

Request a free quote Call us

A flat wall starts with the substrate

The most common mistake with plastering is rushing. If skim plaster goes onto a dusty, non-absorbent or poorly primed substrate, it peels or cracks — and it shows the moment the wall is first painted. That's why we always clean the substrate, prime it and let it dry, even when that adds a day to the timeline.

The second thing is flatness. We measure with a straight edge, not by eye. In older houses walls are often so uneven that skim plaster alone isn't enough and a levelling coat has to go on first.

What we do

  • Skim plastering walls and ceilings — fine finish ready for paint
  • Levelling walls — smoothing out unevenness and waves
  • Cement-lime renders — base renders on new masonry
  • Renovating renders — for damp walls, with a breathable structure
  • Lime renders — for older and listed buildings
  • Skim coats — full-surface levelling ready for paint or wallpaper
  • Reinforcing mesh — over cracks and joints between materials
  • Corner and window beads — edge protection and neat finishing
  • Repairs after demolition — making good chases and penetrations
  • Priming and preparation — always part of the job

How we proceed

  1. We cover floors, furniture and sockets.
  2. We clean the substrate and remove loose layers and old wallpaper.
  3. We prime it and let it dry.
  4. We fit corner beads and, where needed, reinforcing mesh.
  5. On uneven walls we apply a levelling coat.
  6. We apply the skim plaster and smooth it.
  7. Once dry we sand it and check it with a straight edge and raking light.
  8. We clean up and remove the waste.

Damp walls

If a wall is damp near the floor and the render is falling off, ordinary skim plaster won't solve it — the damp will be back within months. The rising damp has to be cut off first (damp coursing or injection) and then a renovating render used, which lets the masonry breathe. We'll spot this at the survey and propose a solution.

Quick facts

  • Duration: a room usually 2–4 days
  • Price: depends on the state of the substrate
  • Always with substrate priming
  • Corner beads and edge protection
  • Renovating renders for damp walls
  • We'll come for a single wall

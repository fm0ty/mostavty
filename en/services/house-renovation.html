House renovation

Family houses, cottages, terraced homes and old properties inherited from grandparents. We handle insulation and roofing as well as layout changes and damp proofing.

Request a free quote Call us

An older house always holds surprises

With houses it's almost never the case that everything is as it looks at first glance. There's no insulation under the floor, the roof timbers are infested, damp rises through the walls, the wiring is aluminium. That's why we survey thoroughly and name the risks before signing the contract, not halfway through.

If something comes to light during demolition — and with old houses it sometimes does — we tell you straight away, show you the solution and the cost, and you decide. We don't press on alone and we don't send you a bill you weren't expecting.

What we most often do on houses

  • Façade insulation including new render
  • A new roof, timbers, insulation and flashings
  • Replacing windows and doors including sills and blinds
  • Layout changes including work on load-bearing walls
  • Damp proofing — damp coursing, injection, renovating renders
  • Complete plumbing and electrical installations
  • New floors including build-up and insulation
  • Loft conversions and dormers
  • Bathrooms, WCs and the utility room
  • Terraces, paving and access paths

Drawings, structural engineer and permits

Work on a load-bearing structure, a loft conversion or a change to the appearance of the house usually needs drawings and a structural engineer's opinion. We work with architects and an engineer, so you don't have to deal with it yourself — we arrange the documentation and the notification to the building authority.

If the house stands in a conservation area or is a listed building, an opinion from the heritage authority is also needed. We'll tell you in advance what that means for the timeline.

Renovating in stages

A house doesn't have to be done all at once. We regularly split a job into stages according to what you have available — first the building envelope (roof, windows, insulation), then the interior. The advantage is that you keep control of the cost; the disadvantage is a longer overall timeline.

Quick facts

  • Duration: usually 3–6 months
  • Price: depends on scope, always quoted
  • Warranty: 60 months on the work
  • Drawings and structural engineer included
  • Possible in stages to suit your budget
  • We handle notifications to the authority

From experience

What we come across in older houses

If you recognise any of these in your own house, tell us at the survey — it affects both the quote and the approach.

Damp walls

Render falling off near the floor and mould in the corners. New render alone won't fix it — the rising damp has to be cut off.

Aluminium wiring

Common in houses from the 1960s–80s. Modern appliances can't be connected to it; usually the whole installation has to be replaced.

Infested roof timbers

Woodworm and rot. Only visible once the roof is opened up. That's why we build a contingency into the quote and say so up front.

No floor insulation

Old houses often have floors laid on clinker or straight onto the ground. Without insulation heat escapes and damp rises.

Unclear structure

Which wall is load-bearing can't be told without drawings. We always bring in a structural engineer before demolition.

Asbestos

In old corrugated roofing and some boards. Disposal follows a legal procedure and is carried out by a licensed firm.
